PLATE III

The counter: one flame, and a cold door

Warm is yours; cold is whatever came up from below. The lighting tells you who to trust before any clue does.
Morningdoor open, grey spill; candle tall. Parties return.
Middaythe house look. Customers.
Eveningpool shrinks, redder. Lending.
Curse candlethe key itself turns verdigris. Tools are light sources.
Nightcandle out: only the page glows. Dues, ledger.
  • The candle is the clock. "Time is the budget" needs no HUD: each inspection melts the tallow a notch toward the next red ring. When it gutters, the day is over. The room physically darkens as your options run out.
  • Tools are lights, not icons. Loupe: a circular warm vignette that sharpens a patch. Curse candle: swaps the key to verdigris so curses fluoresce. Scrying bowl: lights the item from below, wrongly. The reveal is always a lighting change, so the 3D earns its keep.
  • Cold rim is information. Fresh loot arrives with the cold edge still on it and warms over days in stock. Something on your shelf that is still cold-rimmed after a week is telling you something.

MINE AGAIN
  • Uncertainty is ink. An estimate is a blot on a number line: wide and pale when you know nothing, tight and dark after a tool. No percentage ever appears in the shop; percentages belong to Heaven.
  • Unasked questions are listed. Greyed "?" clue lines show what a tool would tell you and what it costs in candle, so the decision is on the page.
  • Haggling is marginal arithmetic. Offers stack as crossings-out in the margin with a one-line tell in the shopkeeper's hand. The patience meter is how hard the customer's quill-strokes get.
  • The pastel tab. The Board is a sticky flag someone else stuck in your book. It counts down. It is the only rounded corner in the shop.

Terms are stickers

Each term carried into next season is a pastel label stuck on the shop: on the shelf, the till, the candle. A long career visibly accretes them, like a laptop lid. Your shop's mess is legible at a glance.

Things come back

An item you have owned before returns with a tiny knotted thread drawn through its provenance, one knot per time. The first buy-back should feel like recognising a face.

Skeleton screens

Heaven's loading placeholders are literal skeletons in the grey-bar style. The only place the two worlds' vocabularies accidentally agree.

Transitions & feedback verbs

  • Page turn between ledger tabs; the candle flame leans with the draught.
  • Stamp slam for every committed decision; ink bleeds for half a second, the coins on the counter jump.
  • Ink dries as the confirm: wet entries can be struck, dry ones are history.
  • The cold arrives first. Before a customer is visible the door-blue spills along the counter and the flame bends away from it.
  • Toast intrusion. Investor messages slide in with a soft-shadowed bounce that belongs to another product, and cast lilac light on the page.
  • Scorch is reserved for the wyrm: paper browning from the edges, used once per lost career.
PLATE VIII

Sound mood

Same split as the picture: the shop is close-miked and dry; Heaven is reverb and licensing-free optimism.

The shop

  • Room tone, no music by default: flame, timber ticking, rain when the door is open.
  • Quill on vellum for every UI write; a scratchier nib when the news is bad.
  • Coins on oak: counted, never jingled. Fewer coins sound lonelier.
  • The door bell is a small cracked one. Each customer type rings it differently.
  • Stamp: a wet thud with a desk rattle. The same thud lands on the notice board when a party dies.
  • Loupe crack when it meets a strong enchantment; the candle gutters when the day runs out.
  • Under the floor: once or twice a day, something large and far down. Never explained, never louder.
  • A single hurdy-gurdy or viol drone creeps in only during the pre-vacation rush.

Startup Heaven

  • Explainer-video ukulele, claps and glockenspiel. Major key, 120 bpm, royalty-free forever.
  • A choir, but side-chained and bit-crushed into a notification chirp.
  • Harp glissando as hold music while the board "aligns".
  • KPI tiles count up with a soft marimba tick.
  • Angel voice: not speech, a warm many-voiced chord with a mouse-click on top.
  • Everything is too clean: no room, no noise floor. After 25 minutes of candle hiss the silence itself is the punchline.

The wyrm

  • No voice. A sub-bass breath so low the coins on the counter buzz.
  • In Heaven: the ukulele loop ducks for one bar whenever the orange dot appears, then resumes as if nothing happened.
  • The typing indicator has a sound: three slow talon taps on a very large keyboard.
  • When the candle goes out at night, the last thing audible is the same breath. It was the room tone all along.
PLATE IX

Rules the direction was missing

Things the direction implied but did not yet answer; all three are now rules.

Legibility under candlelight

  • Noir fails when players squint. Rule: no decision text ever sits on wood or darkness; it sits on vellum at iron-gall contrast (about 11:1). Darkness is for mood, the page is for reading.
  • Fell is rough below about 15 px. Table figures and small labels need a floor size, and the italic "hand" must never carry numbers the player acts on.
  • Verdigris vs. jelly-green vs. amber fails for some colour-blind players: every trait colour needs a paired woodcut glyph (drop, rune, eye, chain).

Who the shopkeeper is, visually

  • The player has no stats and should have no face: only hands at the counter edge and a handwriting. The violet-gall italic is the character.
  • A cover image for itch.io: the title page above, cropped to the three balls with one pastel sticker on it. The whole pitch in one frame.
  • The vacation between seasons needs its own third look. Suggestion: the shop with the shutters closed and daylight leaking in; dust-sheet white, the first un-candlelit view of the room, and it is smaller than you thought.
