# D-AR7QAXJTT2FNYDPN Four fixed slices, then playtests shape the rest

## Context
Two weeks is enough for a playable game only if the parts that make it exist come first, and the rest is likely to change once it can be played.

## Decision
The plan has four fixed slices (visual groundwork, skeleton, one shop day, a season) that are never cut, followed by a playtest gate. The later slices are provisional and are reordered, reshaped or replaced by what the playtest gates show. If time slips, polish is cut first, then reputation, then the richness of provenance.

## Why
Without the fixed slices there is no game, so they cannot be traded away. Planning the later slices firmly would commit to guesses that a playtest is about to overturn. A fixed cut order means slipping never needs a fresh negotiation.

## Consequences
The first milestone ends at the first playtest. Later slices live in the backlog until a gate shapes them.
