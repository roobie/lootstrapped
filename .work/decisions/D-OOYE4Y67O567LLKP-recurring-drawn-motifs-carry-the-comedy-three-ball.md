# D-OOYE4Y67O567LLKP Recurring drawn motifs carry the comedy: three balls, terms as stickers, two names for every death, the dragon as a presence, a faceless shopkeeper

## Context
Grim-comic (D-IGZD65FTBWFT5FBJ) carried by copy alone wears out by the third season, and the board, the dragon and the shopkeeper all need a visual identity that fits the look of D-AW7FZBF73LOHPLXO.

## Decision
The comedy rides on a few recurring drawn motifs. The pawnbroker's three balls are the logo, the favicon and the loader, and in Heaven they become the three angels' avatar dots. Charter terms carried into the next season appear as pastel stickers on the shop and accumulate over a career. Every death has two names, held in a fixed glossary from shop words to board words, so the dissolved are churned. The dragon stays offstage as a presence indicator: "1 anonymous wyrm is viewing", and a typing indicator that never resolves. The shopkeeper has no face, only hands and handwriting.

## Why
A joke built into the visual system compounds with every season where a written one repeats. The three balls tie the shop's oldest sign to the board's three angels and three grades, so one mark serves both worlds. Stickers make the inherited mess of graded renewals (D-G5VL7QCXV5IGX4IY) visible where the player spends their time, so Heaven reaches the shop only through its consequences. The glossary turns the same deaths into people below and a metric above, which is the satire, and it keeps the shop side unsoftened. A presence indicator lets the dragon be felt before it is seen, in the idiom of collaboration software, which keeps it offstage without forgetting it. The shopkeeper has no stats (D-YX3IUCV2YUN7CT7X), and a face would be a character the player is not; hands and a handwriting let the player be the shopkeeper.

## Consequences
The glossary is content, so it lives in data rows like any other words (D-FSULJNRFYK4Z43SE). Shop objects need places where stickers can land. The counter scene never shows the shopkeeper above the wrists.
