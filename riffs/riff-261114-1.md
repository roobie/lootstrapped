# Riff 2026-11-14 #1: Bone Ledger

## The seed
- A game that is **complete within ~2 weeks** of AI-assisted development, then extended and polished incrementally.
- You run a **pawnshop for an adventuring guild**. Parties bring back loot, and you appraise it, haggle, sell it on, and lend gear to the next party.
- **You never see the dungeon**, only what comes back out. Items carry their stories.
- Runs are **seasons** (~30 days).
- Builds on skelly-assets (weapons, armour, props as `.glb`), its showcase turntable, and deadvox's lessons in browser games.
- Deceptively complex yet simple.

## Decisions
- **Tone: grim-comic.** Being dissolved by a great dungeon jelly is just another day on the job.
- **Bounded seasons.** A season is the working period between the guild's collective vacations, when no adventurer works (as in Swedish *industrisemester*). Bounding the season gives it a natural end and a reason to come back.
- **Presentation: a mix.** A 3D scene where it carries the feel (the counter, the item, the customer). Everything else is UI.
- **Scope: start simple, stay extensible.** The counter comes first. Anything beyond it plugs in as data and panels, with no rewrite.

## Bounces (proposals, not decisions)
- **The pre-vacation rush.** Late in the season, adventurers sell gear cheap to fund their holidays, and parties take on riskier delves for one last big haul. The tension builds by itself, with no extra mechanics.
- **Closing Day.** The ledger is settled on the last day: dues are paid, and unsold stock goes into storage at a cost or is dumped. A season is then a complete, scorable run.
- **Seasons chain into a career.** The spring season, a vacation, then the autumn season. The vacation is the meta layer: shop upgrades, and gossip about who didn't come back.
- **Customers are skelly mobs.** Whoever is at the counter is a rigged mob with clips (idle, impatient, pleased), so the asset pipeline also provides the cast.
- **The guild notice board as the first extension.** It shows departures, deaths and bounties as plain data, and sets up the guild hall later.
- **Information is the game.** Every item has a true value and hidden traits (cursed, forged, enchanted, stolen). Appraisal gives an estimate with an error bar, and skill or tools narrow it (as with riff-261106-2's power estimate).
- **Two sides of the counter.** Buy cheap from returning parties and sell dear to customers. Each side is its own haggle against its own kind of desperation.
- **Lending is investment.** Gear lent to a departing party raises its odds of survival. If the party dies, the gear is lost to the dungeon, and might turn up later on someone else's corpse, so you buy back your own sword.
- **Provenance.** Every item keeps an event log ("lent to the Ashwardens, day 4; recovered from the Ashwardens' last member, day 9"). That's where the emergent story comes from, at almost no content cost.
- **Day loop:** morning: parties return (buy); midday: customers (sell); evening: outfit departing parties (lend or sell on credit); night: dues, ledger, upgrades.
- **The dungeon is an offscreen simulation.** Party power against depth, seeded and deterministic, with the gear you supplied in the formula. It is never rendered.
- **Haggling as reading people.** A hidden reservation price, a patience meter, and personality tells. Short and readable, not a minigame grind.
- **Reputation.** Fleecing a desperate party gets you cheap stock now and fewer sellers later. Fences offer stolen goods at a reputation risk.
- **Pressure.** Rising guild dues or debt give the season a clock and make it possible to lose.
- **Moddable from day one.** Item kinds, traits, parties, customers and dungeons are data rows. The `.glb` meshes come from skelly-assets.

## Open questions
- What does "winning" a season mean: hitting a profit target, surviving the dues, or a graded score?
- Is the shopkeeper a character with stats (appraisal, haggling, reputation), or does the player's skill do all the work?
- Who owns the shop: you, or a landlord or guild you owe?
- Season length: ~30 days, or shorter, so a season fits one sitting?
